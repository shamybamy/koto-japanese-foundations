1. 平板 Heiban - flat
	- one mora words
		- flat/accentless
	- words more than one mora
		- slightly low -> high
			- time = ji-ka-n
				- ji(low)-ka(high)-n(high)
2. 頭高 Atamadaka - head-high
	- first mora high, then all low
		- basically same as heiban, but more drastic
			- everyday = ma-i-ni-chi
				- ma(high)-i(low)-ni(low)-chi(low)
3. 中高 Nakadaka - middle-high
	- first mora low, then high then low
		- the drop from high to low is bigger than the raise from low to high
			- one = hi-to-tsu
				- hi(low)-to(high)-tsu(low)
4. 尾高 Odaka - tail-high
	- first mora low, then all high
		- two = fu-ta-tsu
			- fu(low)-ta(high)-tsu(high)

Rule of thumb:
1. Rule 1: if the first mora is low, the second is high, and vice versa
2. Rule 2: once you go low, stay low
3. Rule 3: if in doubt, guess
	- guess 平板 (start low, then go high and stay high).

For particles like が (na):
- For 平板 words, the particle pitch is high.
	- company = ka(low)-i(high)-sh(high)-a(high)-*na(high)*
- For words the other three pitch accents, the particle pitch is low.
	- world = se(high)-ka(low)-i(low)-*na(low)*
	- japan = ni(low)-ho(high)-n(low)-*na(low)*

Sentences:
1.  Pitch stays high across word boundaries until it reaches a downstep
2. After a downstep, the next rise in pitch is not as high as the downstep
3. Words you want to emphasize should have a somewhat higher pitch
